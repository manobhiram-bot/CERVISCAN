About Cervical Rib

What is a Cervical Rib?

A cervical rib is a supernumerary (extra) rib arising from the seventh cervical vertebra (C7). It is a congenital anomaly located above the normal first rib. While most individuals possess 12 pairs of ribs, approximately 0.2% to 0.5% (1 in 200 to 500 people) are born with this accessory skeletal structure.

Why is it Clinically Significant?

Although frequently asymptomatic, an accessory cervical rib can compress nerves or key vascular channels crossing from the neck into the arm. The primary clinical condition resulting from this friction or compression is known as Thoracic Outlet Syndrome (TOS).

Common Symptom Profile

Pain radiating down the neck, shoulder, arm, or hand.
Paresthesia (numbness, tingling, or "pins-and-needles") in digits.
Weakness of the fingers or general hand grip fatigue.
Coldness, pallor, or blood flow restriction in the arm (Vascular TOS).

How CerviScan Helps

CerviScan automates the analysis of medical chest and cervical X-ray films using a deep learning classifier. It detects the presence of left, right, or bilateral (both sides) cervical ribs. By flagging these anomalies during routine clinical screening, it accelerates orthopedic or neurological diagnosis.

Help & Documentation

1. X-Ray Image Standard

For accurate predictions, upload only standard monochromatic chest or cervical spine X-ray films. The image must clearly cover the C7 vertebra region at the base of the neck.

Avoid uploading color portraits, photographic scans of reports, patient photos, or charts. The AI system runs internal validation filters (histogram contrast, brightness standard dev, and background dark-ratio checks) and will reject non-compliant files.

2. Deep Learning Classifier

The diagnostic logic is powered by a MobileNet-based deep convolutional neural network trained on a dataset of labeled cervical X-ray examinations containing bilateral, unilateral (left/right), and normal vertebrae structures.

The model calculates probabilities across four classes: Bicrib (Bilateral Ribs), Left Cervical Rib, Right Cervical Rib, and Normal.

3. Clinical Reports

After executing a prediction, you can download a formatted report. The report integrates patient credentials, diagnostic findings, and clinical disclaimers. You can directly print it to paper or save as PDF via your browser's PDF engine.
